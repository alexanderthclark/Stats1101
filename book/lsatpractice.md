# Statistical Reasoning Practice

These optional exercises point to LSAT® Logical Reasoning questions and use
them to practice recognizing statistical ideas inside short arguments. The
questions were not written as statistics exercises, which is precisely what
makes them useful: you must decide which statistical principle matters before
applying it.

```{admonition} External content and access
:class: important

This book does **not** reproduce the question text, answer choices, answer key,
or explanations. Each exercise links to the question on 7Sage. Access to the
complete question may require a 7Sage account, a subscription, and/or LawHub
Advantage. If you have lawful access through another source, use the PrepTest,
section, and question identifier instead.

These exercises are optional because access is controlled by an external
service. LSAT® is a registered trademark of the Law School Admission Council,
Inc. (LSAC), which is not affiliated with and does not endorse this site.
```

For each exercise:

1. Open the linked question and choose an answer before revealing the
   explanation.
2. Return here and answer the statistical follow-up in your own words.
3. Refer to the question by its identifier; do not copy its text or answer
   choices into your work.

## Rates, averages, and denominators

```{admonition} External practice: PT155.S4.Q21
:class: note lsat-practice

[Complete PT155.S4.Q21 on 7Sage](https://7sage.com/question/PT155.S4.Q21).

**Course connection:** {ref}`center_and_spread` — weighted averages and subgroup
comparisons.

Write the overall percentage as a weighted average of the two group
percentages. Explain why the unreported group's percentage must lie on a
particular side of the overall percentage.
```

```{admonition} External practice: PT148.S1.Q2
:class: note lsat-practice

[Complete PT148.S1.Q2 on 7Sage](https://7sage.com/question/PT148.S1.Q2).

**Course connection:** {ref}`getting_started` — interpreting percentages and
their denominators.

Write a general identity connecting a subgroup's share, the total size of the
population, and the subgroup count. Identify which quantity would have to be
known before a change in share could establish the same proportional change in
the count.
```

```{admonition} External practice: PT121.S4.Q25
**Course connection:** {ref}`the_histogram` — observational units and variables.

Identify the unit summarized by the evidence and the unit described by the
```

## Probability and chance

```{admonition} External practice: PT146.S3.Q5
:class: note lsat-practice

[Complete PT146.S3.Q5 on 7Sage](https://7sage.com/question/PT146.S3.Q5).

**Course connection:** {ref}`probability` — the addition rule and overlapping
events.

Represent the two reported groups as sets $A$ and $B$. Write the general
addition rule for $P(A \text{ or } B)$ and identify the unstated assumption
about their overlap.
```

